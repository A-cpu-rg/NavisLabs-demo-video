## 5. Music requirements

- One continuous score, `public/music/final/v4/score.wav`: WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit, stereo, exactly 100.0 s, starting at 0.000 s, silent from 98.6 s.
- Loudness: score alone: about −16 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (the film applies −7 dB and ducks −9 dB under every voice line).
- Arc:
  - 0–12 s — Restrained, almost silent
  - 12–22 s — Subtle brand emergence
  - 22–45 s — Human / organizational
  - 45–68 s — Technical developer momentum
  - 68–82 s — Convergence / shared understanding
  - 82–94 s — Conviction / moat
  - 94–100 s — Resolution → silence
- Full rests: 10.0–10.45 s (before NavisLabs), 40.95–41.2 s (Approve & send), 98.6–100 s. Pull back to −18 dB during the refusal (60.9–62.1 s).
- Forbidden: generic corporate music (ukulele, claps, 'inspiring' piano-pop); trailer drums, booms, braams, impacts; excessive risers or reverse swells; constant whooshes; 'AI' clichés: fast glittery arpeggios, robotic vocoder; vocals or choirs with words; a hit on the NavisLabs reveal; anything louder than the voice.
- The voice is always dominant: leave 120–350 Hz and 2–4 kHz open under speech; no lead melody under a voice line.
- Full brief with section-by-section instrumentation, picture anchors and sync points: `navislabs_v4_music_production_brief.json`.

## 8. What you need to record

- **If you clone your voice (recommended for consistency):** for a Professional Voice Clone, at least 30 minutes of your own clean speech (2–3 hours is best). Use one quiet, dead room, one microphone 15–20 cm away, consistent level, no music or other voices, and no long silences. Read in the film's register: calm, explanatory, unhurried. An Instant Voice Clone needs 1–2 minutes but is less faithful.
- **Or record the 26 lines yourself:** read each line to picture against `out/navislabs-v4-picture-lock-1080p.mp4`, 3 takes per line, 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV, same room and microphone throughout, about 1 s of room tone before each take.
- Confirm the pronunciation of 'Navis' / 'NavisLabs' and say it the same way everywhere.
- Nothing else to record. Music and SFX are produced separately.

## 10. What Claude will automatically assemble

Already automatic in the project:

- Each slot plays its final file if present, else temp, else silence. Delivering a file is a file drop, not a code change.
- Every voice line is placed at its start time and cut at its hard end. Music ducks −9 dB around every line with 0.15 s attack and 0.5 s release, and dips at the planned silences.
- The temp data-flow texture mutes itself once the final score is present.
- `mode: "final"` refuses to render until all 26 lines and the score are final.
- `npm run validate:film` checks final takes against their hard ends and the 48 kHz sample rate, plus the SFX categories and silences.

When you deliver files, Claude will:

1. QC every take: format, length against the hard end, leading and trailing silence, peaks, clipping, noise.
2. Convert to 48 kHz / 24-bit mono WAV and trim heads and tails to ≤ 50 ms, keeping the breath on 'after a short breath' lines.
3. Split passage takes into V01–V26 at their pauses.
4. Set each line's measured speech end, so the ducking follows your real voice.
5. Align the phrase-sync lines (V03, V06, V14, V15, V19, V21, V22, V23, V25, V26) to their picture beats if a take drifts by more than its tolerance.
6. Check the score's length, silences and loudness.
7. Rerun every validator.
8. Render the 1080p final, measure loudness, peaks and clipping, then render the 4K master after your approval.
